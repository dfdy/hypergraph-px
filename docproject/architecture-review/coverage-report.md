The current local workflow is reconstructed in substantially more detail than future collaboration/integrations.

Coverage is based on a mechanical whole-document scan plus targeted semantic examination of requirements, authority, core model, protocols, ADRs, subsystem contracts and assurance. A heading index does not prove equal depth of human inspection for every line; broad source disposition is not independent validation of every retained claim. Historical proof/test reports were read as evidence, not rerun. Existing archival claim counts were not re-audited as a semantic-completeness proof.

Known reconstruction limits: no adopted federation causal/authority/conflict protocol; no complete multi-owner source/occurrence writer; no general same-identity branch Merge; no full rights/delegation algebra or separately adopted named-holder/mandate mechanism; no portable package byte schemas or general restore; no deterministic academic compiler/writer profile, XML/JSON schema or complete citation capture representation; no live agent/MCP/provider job protocol. Future diagrams show required boundaries and open transitions, not invented messages or persisted types.

The DFD context and Level 1 balance eight explicitly named boundary channels. Lower DFDs are scoped operation zooms, not claims to exhaust every operation inside the grouped Level-1 circles. Conceptual and physical data views are separate. All process leaves are reachable through family diagrams and catalogues, but only ordering-sensitive workflows receive sequence/state detail; forcing all leaves into every notation would add no evidence.

The source contains compound normative passages beyond the 31 seeds. A defensible percentage of all atomic obligations cannot be computed without owner-reviewed decomposition. See [FIND-018](findings/HPX-FIND-018.md). The right conclusion is complete inventory coverage with explicit semantic gaps, not “the entire future system is fully specified.”
